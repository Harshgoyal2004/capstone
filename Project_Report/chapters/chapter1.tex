\section{Background}

The exponential development of \ac{ai} and \ac{ml} in the sphere of healthcare has enabled unprecedented opportunities in the area of early disease detection and personalized treatment. Diseases such as cardiovascular diseases, diabetes mellitus, and neurological disorders, including Parkinson's disease, have posed a severe challenge on the international level. The World Health Organization has established that cardiovascular diseases remain the number one cause of mortality across the globe, resulting in about 17.9 million deaths each year. There are more than 500 million individuals with type 2 diabetes, while 10 million suffer from Parkinson's disease around the world. Moreover, there is an upward trend in the spread of the disease linked to ageing populations.

The early diagnosis of these diseases is known to be the best prevention measure, yet preliminary tests remain costly and out of reach for many due to geographical limitations and lack of specialists. Thus, the incorporation of \ac{ai}-based software to preliminary screenings is expected to contribute to early risk assessment. This project seeks to fill the gap in the market by developing an \textit{AI Health Screening Assistant} that will perform medical intake conversations based on three pretrained \ac{dl} models and Google Gemini \ac{llm}.

\subsection{Current Systems and Existing Solutions}

In the current scenario, existing solutions to automated health screening usually belong to any of the following three categories. First, there are single-disease screening devices that are prevalent in the industry, in which the employed \ac{ai} health screening tools are only applicable in a single disease domain. As an example, \ac{ecg} analyzers such as AliveCor's KardiaMobile device can exclusively identify atrial fibrillation, while MySugr app only focuses on diabetes. Consequently, there are limitations as far as their scope in multi-domain health screening is concerned. The second category of existing solutions consists of symptom-based diagnosis apps, such as Ada Health and Infermedica's Symptom Checker, which employ decision-tree/probabilistic reasoning techniques to generate potential diseases from user-reported symptoms. They do not leverage \ac{dl} models or biomarker datasets. Finally, there are an overwhelming number of research-grade \ac{ml} models. In particular, a large volume of literature proves highly accurate \ac{ml} classifiers for individual diseases, notably including \ac{ecg} arrhythmia detection \cite{guhdar2024}, diabetes prediction \cite{daza2024}, and Parkinson's disease through voice analysis \cite{islam2024}, yet these models have not been deployed as products.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Systems}

Some of the major limitations of existing tools are as follows. For one, the existing frameworks operate on fragmented architectures, making them heavily biased towards the single-disease paradigm and resulting in a gap in commercial products that allow simultaneous cardiac, metabolic, and motor screening. Additionally, the tools are often devoid of conversational intake. Since they require input data manually entered either through a form or a sophisticated app, this makes them inaccessible to non-technical users, whereas a conversational \ac{ai} solution allows overcoming this hurdle. Moreover, there is no explainability to the results, as existing tools only present a clear risk flag without further explanations, thereby failing to educate users about their health risk factors. Finally, these systems lack a closed-loop \ac{llm} architecture, in which an \ac{llm} can facilitate data collection symmetrically and later make sense of the mathematical results of downstream \ac{ml} models in comprehensible language.

\subsection{Relevant Technologies and Concepts}

Some of the technologies and concepts relevant to this project include Deep Learning insofar as the three predictive models (\acl{cnn}-based ECGNet and tabular \ac{dnn}-based DiabetesNet, ParkinsonNet) are concerned, Signal Processing in terms of preprocessing steps in \ac{ecg} classification, feature extraction in ParkinsonNet, FastAPI for building an \ac{api} server, Streamlit for frontend user interface, and Large Language Model --- more specifically, Google's Gemini for facilitating natural conversations between the \ac{ai} agent and the user.

\subsection{Theoretical Foundations}

The theoretical basis for each predictive model is described below. ECGNet (Cardiac Screening): The SE \ac{net} architecture along with multiscale convolutional features extraction provides theoretical justification for this deep learning classifier's ability to classify complex \ac{ecg} patterns at varying scales. On the other hand, DiabetesNet (Metabolic Screening): The architecture is a fully connected tabular \ac{dnn} trained on the Pima Indians Diabetes Dataset and configured to implement Binary Cross-Entropy loss with logit function via Batch Normalization and Dropout layers. Finally, ParkinsonNet (Motor Screening): The theoretical basis for the \ac{dnn} model is a set of widely acknowledged acoustic biomarkers associated with the presence of Parkinson's disease and capturing deviations in jitter, shimmer, and harmonics-to-noise ratio.

\section{Motivation}

\subsection{Relevance}

The three diseases in question have something in common: pre-symptomatic screening greatly improves their patients' lives but lacks sufficient early-stage screening instruments that are available to people at large. Combining several different diseases in one conversational \ac{ai} framework can save time, raise awareness and become complementary to clinical practice.

\subsection{Practical and Scientific Motivation}

Practically speaking, this project showcases a full-fledged implementation of the \ac{ai} application, which encompasses a lot of elements ranging from deep learning inference and signal processing to language models orchestration and web UI design. On the other hand, in terms of scientific motivation, this work introduces an innovative \textit{double-pass \ac{llm}} paradigm, where the language model acts both as data collector and as the results interpretation mechanism, yet does not make any clinical predictions itself.

\subsection{Aspect of Innovation}

The unique feature of the architecture is closed-loop \ac{llm}-ML pipeline, when \ac{llm} collects data conversationally, produces a structured \texttt{<MODEL\_INPUT>} XML block, which is further intercepted, processed using three separate deep learning models and reassembled into a \texttt{<MODEL\_OUTPUT>} block, passed back to \ac{llm} which finally generates result descriptions. Thus, no medical risks are generated by the language model, and all quantitative values are produced exclusively by the \ac{ml} part of the architecture.

\section{Scope of the Project}

\subsection{Functional Scope}

The AI Health Screening Assistant enables users to enter data via a conversational chat with Gemini \ac{llm} on three different disease domains. For the purpose of collecting such data, it provides two different types of files uploads --- CSV waveforms for cardiac screening and WAV voice recordings for Parkinson's screening. As for metabolism-related screening, it allows users to gather information about eight different precise medical biomarkers via conversation. After this information collection process is done, the assistant implements a hierarchical triaging of diseases according to their severity level, allocating patients for routine examination, checking certain symptoms or even urgent treatment, depending on their condition and providing result explanations for every patient.

\subsection{Non-Functional Scope}

Firstly, it needs to be noted that the entire architecture adheres to a very strict set of non-functional requirements, as it was originally conceived as a screening tool, which does not generate diagnostic assertions. Besides, in order to provide fluid interactions between users, the average latency of the system has been rigorously optimized by using pre-loading sequences executed right at the moment of server startup. The stability of the system has been provided by means of native ability to failover between different Gemini models at will and thus maintaining API availability throughout the interaction process. Finally, since there are no specific requirements for front end implementation, everything is available via any browser without having to install anything locally.

\subsection{Technical Scope}

The whole project consists of Python code written in version 3.10+. The backend \ac{api} is implemented using FastAPI $\geq$ 0.115 and \ac{ml} components run on PyTorch $\geq$ 2.0 framework. The frontend is developed with the help of Streamlit 1.38.
\subsection{Project Boundaries}

In establishing its absolute operational bounds, it must be acknowledged that the system is definitively not intended for live clinical deployment nor formal regulatory medical submission. Clinically, because the diagnostic models were trained solely on extremely specific quantitative population datasets, their resulting inferences may not reliably generalise evenly across all demographic representations. Furthermore, technical scope limitations dictate that real-time audio recording capability is deliberately not supported, compelling users to instead supply pre-recorded WAV files for acoustic analysis. By the same token, advanced multi-lead \ac{ecg} analysis is not supported; the extraction algorithms are strictly tuned such that only single-lead voltage waveform CSV files are accepted. Finally, ensuring total baseline privacy compliance, the application architecture guarantees that the system intrinsically does not maintain or store patient data, nor does it implement any backend mechanisms geared towards persistent user session management.
