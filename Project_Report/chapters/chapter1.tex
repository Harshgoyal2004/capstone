\section{Background}

The rapid advancement of \ac{ai} and \ac{ml} technologies in the healthcare domain has opened transformative possibilities for early disease detection and personalised patient care. Chronic diseases such as cardiovascular disorders, diabetes mellitus, and neurodegenerative conditions like Parkinson's disease represent a significant global health burden. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), cardiovascular diseases are the leading cause of death globally, claiming approximately 17.9 million lives annually. Type 2 diabetes affects over 500 million adults worldwide, while Parkinson's disease is estimated to affect 10 million people globally, with incidence increasing rapidly due to ageing populations.

Early and accurate screening is widely recognised as the most effective strategy to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with these conditions. However, preliminary diagnostic screening remains inaccessible to a large portion of the global population due to the cost of clinical consultations, geographical constraints, and specialist shortages. The integration of \ac{ai}-driven tools into preliminary health screening can potentially democratise access to early-stage risk assessment.

This project addresses this unmet need by building an \textit{AI Health Screening Assistant} — a conversational platform that employs three pretrained \ac{dl} models alongside a Google Gemini \ac{llm} to conduct structured medical intake interviews and produce simultaneous risk assessments for cardiac arrhythmia, diabetes, and Parkinson's disease.

\subsection{Current Systems and Existing Solutions}

existing approaches to automated health screening generally fall into one of three categories. Firstly, \textbf{single-disease screening tools} dominate the landscape, where deployed \ac{ai} health tools focus strictly on a single disease domain. For example, \ac{ecg} analysis tools such as AliveCor's KardiaMobile exclusively detect atrial fibrillation, while applications like MySugr focus solely on diabetes management; consequently, these specialised tools lack the capability to provide holistic, multi-domain screening from a single interaction. Secondly, there are \textbf{symptom checkers driven by rule-based logic}, such as Ada Health and Infermedica's Symptom Checker, which apply decision-tree or probabilistic reasoning to suggest possible conditions based on user-reported symptoms. However, they generally do not incorporate \ac{dl} models or process raw biomarker data, thereby limiting their clinical informativeness. Finally, there exists a multitude of \textbf{research-grade \ac{ml} models}. While a vast body of published literature demonstrates high-performance \ac{ml} algorithms for individual disease classification---particularly for \ac{ecg} arrhythmia detection \cite{guhdar2024}, diabetes prediction \cite{daza2024}, and Parkinson's voice analysis \cite{islam2024}---these remain predominantly confined as research artifacts lacking accessible, user-friendly deployment interfaces.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Systems}

The primary limitations identified in these existing solutions stem from their fragmented architectures. Their severe \textbf{single-disease focus} means that there is currently no commercially available tool capable of simultaneously performing cardiac, metabolic, and motor risk screening from a unified interface. Compounding this issue is a widespread \textbf{lack of conversational intake}. Because existing tools require rigid manual form filling or complex app-based data entry, they inherently alienate non-technical patients, whereas a conversational \ac{ai}-driven approach would dramatically lower this barrier to entry. Furthermore, there is a pervasive \textbf{absence of explainability}; most deployed systems simply return a stark binary risk flag without offering any contextual explanation. This ultimately fails to empower users with the required knowledge to understand their own health risk factors or to subsequently take informed action. Most critically, these systems suffer from having \textbf{no closed-loop \ac{llm} architecture}. The foundational concept of using an \ac{llm} not only to collect structured data symmetrically but to subsequently interpret the quantitative mathematical outputs of downstream \ac{ml} models into digestible, patient-facing language stands as a distinct novelty that has yet to be implemented in any known commercial diagnostic product.

\subsection{Relevant Technologies and Concepts}

This project draws on several key technological domains. \textbf{Deep Learning} underpins all three predictive models — specifically 1D \acl{cnn}s for \ac{ecg} signal classification and tabular \ac{dnn}s for structured clinical data. \textbf{Signal Processing} is used extensively in the \ac{ecg} preprocessing pipeline (Butterworth bandpass filtering, R-peak detection) and audio feature extraction (pYIN pitch estimation, jitter/shimmer computation, nonlinear dynamics). \textbf{Large Language Models} — specifically Google Gemini — provide the conversational intelligence for medical data collection and result interpretation. \textbf{FastAPI} and \textbf{Streamlit} constitute the backend \ac{api} layer and frontend \ac{ui} respectively.

\subsection{Theoretical Foundations}

The theoretical underpinning of each predictive subsystem is as follows:

\textbf{ECGNet (Cardiac Screening)} is modeled upon the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) mechanism \cite{senet2018} alongside multi-scale convolutional feature extraction architectures, inherently enabling the model to accurately capture complex \ac{ecg} morphological patterns across drastically different temporal resolutions simultaneously. Conversely, \textbf{DiabetesNet (Metabolic Screening)} engages a fully connected tabular \ac{dnn} strictly enforced with Batch Normalization and Dropout configurations, identically trained using Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss leveraging the Pima Indians Diabetes Dataset. Finally, \textbf{ParkinsonNet (Motor Screening)} mathematically exploits well-established acoustic biomarkers indicative of Parkinson's disease---namely capturing deviations in jitter, shimmer, alongside the harmonic-to-noise ratio---all of which holistically reflect the physiological dysphonia (voice disorder) acting as the predominant characteristic of the pathological condition \cite{little2008}.

\section{Motivation}

\subsection{Problem Significance}

The three diseases targeted by this system — cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and Parkinson's disease — share a common characteristic: early, pre-symptomatic detection dramatically improves quality of life and clinical outcomes, yet accessible early-stage screening tools remain limited. Integrating multi-disease screening into a single conversational \ac{ai} platform can reduce diagnostic delays, improve awareness, and complement existing clinical workflows.

\subsection{Practical and Academic Motivation}

From a practical standpoint, this project demonstrates how a full-stack \ac{ai} application — combining \ac{dl} inference, signal processing, \ac{llm} orchestration, and a modern web interface — can be designed, trained, and deployed by a small team. Academically, it explores a novel \textit{double-pass \ac{llm}} architecture in which the language model serves both as a data collection agent and as a result interpretation layer, without fabricating clinical predictions.

\subsection{Innovation Aspect}

The key innovation is the \textbf{closed-loop LLM-ML pipeline}: the \ac{llm} collects data conversationally, emits a structured \texttt{<MODEL\_INPUT>} XML block which the backend intercepts, runs three independent \ac{dl} models, formats a \texttt{<MODEL\_OUTPUT>} block, and returns it to the \ac{llm} for patient-facing explanation. This prevents hallucination of medical risk values and ensures all quantitative outputs originate from the \ac{dl} models, not the language model.

\section{Scope of the Project}

\subsection{Functional Scope}

The AI Health Screening Assistant facilitates conversational medical intake via the Gemini \ac{llm} across three distinct disease domains. To support this functionality, the system accepts uploads and conducts direct sequential inference on \ac{ecg} waveform CSV files tailored for cardiac screening, alongside parsing voice recording WAV files targeting Parkinson's screening. For metabolic assessment, it seamlessly orchestrates the collection of eight precise clinical biomarkers directly via chat. Having compiled this robust clinical profile, the assistant then enforces an automated triage classification hierarchy---sorting conditions into routine, recommended check, or priority review allocations---culminating in the generation of patient-friendly result explanations deeply enriched with contextual clinical recommendations.

\subsection{Non-Functional Scope}

Critically, the architecture operates strictly within rigid non-functional constraints, being fundamentally designed as a \textbf{screening tool only} that explicitly prohibits any generation of diagnostic claims. To ensure fluid user interaction, overall response latency remains heavily optimised through proactive model pre-loading sequences initiated directly at server startup. Backend robustness is guaranteed as the system natively supports dynamic failover routing across multiple Gemini model versions, ensuring uninterrupted \ac{api} availability. Consequently, the entire operational pipeline is delivered frictionlessly, as the frontend experience is completely accessible via any standard web browser without demanding local installation.

\subsection{Technical Scope}

The system is implemented entirely in Python 3.10+, uses PyTorch $\geq$ 2.0 for inference, FastAPI 0.115 for the backend \ac{rest} \ac{api}, and Streamlit 1.38 for the frontend.

\subsection{Project Boundaries}

In establishing its absolute operational bounds, it must be acknowledged that the system is definitively \textbf{not} intended for live clinical deployment nor formal regulatory medical submission. Clinically, because the diagnostic models were trained solely on extremely specific quantitative population datasets, their resulting inferences may not reliably generalise evenly across all demographic representations. Furthermore, technical scope limitations dictate that real-time audio recording capability is deliberately not supported, compelling users to instead supply pre-recorded WAV files for acoustic analysis. By the same token, advanced multi-lead \ac{ecg} analysis is not supported; the extraction algorithms are strictly tuned such that only single-lead voltage waveform CSV files are accepted. Finally, ensuring total baseline privacy compliance, the application architecture guarantees that the system intrinsically does not maintain or store patient data, nor does it implement any backend mechanisms geared towards persistent user session management.
