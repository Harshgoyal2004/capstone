\section{Conclusion}

This project has successfully designed, implemented, and evaluated an \ac{ai}-powered conversational health screening platform that simultaneously assesses cardiac arrhythmia, diabetes mellitus, and Parkinson's disease risk through a unified web interface. The system makes several notable contributions:

Foremost among these is the introduction of a \textbf{Novel Double-Pass \ac{llm} Architecture}. The closed-loop \ac{llm}-\ac{ml} pipeline, in which Google Gemini contextually collects structured data, triggers strictly deterministic PyTorch inference, and subsequently interprets those pure mathematical model outputs, represents a highly novel approach to \ac{llm}-mediated clinical screening that actively prevents the hallucination of vital medical risk values. Building on this secure architecture is the achievement of \textbf{Multi-Disease Screening in a Single Session}. The seamless integration of three structurally independent disease domains---cardiac, metabolic, and motor---into a single, consolidated conversational session provides a unique patient capability currently absent in globally available public health tools. Furthermore, the architecture succeeds in functioning as a \textbf{Production-Grade Deployment}, effectively demonstrating that a full-stack \ac{ai} application harmonising deep learning inference, digital signal processing, \ac{llm} prompt orchestration, and a responsive web \ac{ui} can be designed, trained, and solidly deployed as a working prototype entirely by an undergraduate engineering cohort. Finally, this system acts as a crucial \textbf{Research-Practice Bridge}; by actively packaging well-established, highly cited research models---namely ECGNet, DiabetesNet, and ParkinsonNet---into an actively deployable application wrapped in a patient-friendly interface, the project tangibly bridges the systemic gap separating isolated academic research artifacts from practical, accessible health technology.

The three \ac{dl} models achieve performance levels consistent with published state-of-the-art on their respective benchmark datasets: ECGNet achieves 60.0\% test accuracy (Macro AUROC 0.7562), DiabetesNet achieves 73.4\% test accuracy (AUROC 0.8170), and ParkinsonNet achieves 97.4\% test accuracy (AUROC 0.9931). The automated triage system correctly classifies screening sessions based on combined risk levels.

The system adheres to strict medical safety principles: it is explicitly positioned as a screening tool rather than a diagnostic system, always recommends professional medical consultation, and never claims diagnostic certainty.

\section{Further Enhancements}

The following enhancements are identified as high-priority directions for future development:

\subsection{Model Improvements}

Looking towards algorithmic progression, addressing the underlying mathematical constraints of the diagnostic models remains paramount. \textbf{ECGNet Regularisation} requires urgent attention to close the severe training-test accuracy gap (98.55\% versus 60.0\%) via stronger data augmentation techniques such as time-warping and amplitude scaling, alongside the adoption of patient-level cross-validation rather than naive record-level splits. Fixing the fundamental \textbf{S-class and F-class Detection} failures will require executing cost-sensitive learning or Synthetic Minority Oversampling Techniques (SMOTE) to mathematically balance the Supraventricular and Fusion beats. To widen generalisability, \textbf{Multi-Ethnic Dataset} integration is mandatory, necessitating the retraining of DiabetesNet upon broader populations such as the Saudi Arabian diabetes dataset \cite{gollapalli2022} or the BRFSS data collection. Similarly, \textbf{ParkinsonNet Dataset Expansion} must incorporate massive global archives, such as the 10,000+ participant mPower mobile Parkinson dataset \cite{dixit2024}, to ensure absolute clinical validity. Finally, migrating towards \textbf{Transformer-Based \ac{ecg} Models} \cite{liu2024} and deploying \textbf{Clinical-Grade Nonlinear Dynamics} equations from validated packages like \texttt{neurokit2} will systematically fortify the platform's diagnostic inference pipeline.

\subsection{System Architecture Enhancements}

Regarding foundational architecture, proactive backend updates are mandated. An immediate \textbf{\ac{llm} SDK Migration} to the newly released \texttt{google.genai} package is necessary to preemptively preserve long-term API functionality upon the deprecation of legacy generative protocols. Clinically, incorporating \textbf{Multi-lead \ac{ecg} Support} would greatly elevate the system's diagnostic authority by transitioning from rudimentary single-lead analysis to holistically comprehensive 12-lead assessments. On the frontend, seamlessly integrating \textbf{Real-time Audio Recording} capabilities directly into the Streamlit interface would significantly streamline patient onboarding by eliminating the current reliance on manual WAV file uploads. Concurrently, building persistent databases to handle \textbf{Continuous Monitoring} and longitudinal tracking will empower users to monitor nuanced risk transversals over elongated timeframes reliably.

\subsection{Security and Compliance Enhancements}

Securing digital health infrastructure dictates immediate privacy integrations. Implementing standard \textbf{Authentication} layers via OAuth2 or JWT protocols is paramount to securely restrict \ac{api} access while ensuring user intake states remain rigidly isolated. Simultaneously, stringent \textbf{Data Encryption} layers must be enforced at rest and consistently in transit, strictly coupled with the immediate automated purging of any uploaded clinical telemetry the second a session completes. To safeguard computational resources against external abuse, deploying robust \textbf{Rate Limiting} measures across production environments is mandatory. Lastly, engineering an immutable, anonymised \textbf{Audit Logging} pipeline will provide essential security monitoring while guaranteeing alignment with stringent global clinical research compliance paradigms.

\subsection{Evaluation and Validation}

To graduate from a working prototype into a certified health screening apparatus, comprehensive validation is necessary. A formal \textbf{Clinical Pilot Study}, rigorously conducted within an official hospital setting operating under appropriate IRB board oversight, must be deployed to critically evaluate the algorithmic screening sensitivity against verified physician diagnoses. Moreover, executing a dedicated \textbf{User Experience Study} encompassing highly diverse patient groups possessing varying degrees of baseline technical literacy will definitively evaluate the system's true conversational comprehensibility testing. Finally, bridging the trust gap between artificial intelligence and human clinicians requires deep \textbf{Explainability (XAI)} interfaces; natively rendering real-time Grad-CAM visualisations mapping directly over patient \ac{ecg} sequences, alongside generating interactive SHAP value decompositions for tabular diabetes metrics, will decisively transform opaque black-box neural predictions into fully transparent, medically actionable clinical insights.
